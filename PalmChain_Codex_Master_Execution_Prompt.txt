MANDATORY ARCHITECTURE AND SAFETY CONSTRAINTS

- Clinical data and raw FHIR resources stay off-chain in encrypted, governed stores.
- Never put names, phone numbers, national identifiers, raw documents, raw FHIR, public object-storage links, stable patient IDs or sensitive clinical narratives on the ledger.
- Fabric is optional for the pilot. Disabled and visibly unavailable is safer than simulated success.
- A production Fabric design requires real multi-organization governance, TLS, certificate lifecycle, identity-aware chaincode, matching endorsement policies, monitoring and tested recovery.
- FHIR is an exchange model, not an automatic security layer.
- Every sensitive request requires server-side authorization using role plus relevant facility, relationship, purpose, consent/basis, sensitivity and emergency context.
- System/facility administrators do not receive routine PHI access by default.
- Ministry analytics are aggregate/de-identified by default; row-level public-health use requires separate authority and controls.
- Unauthenticated emergency access reveals only an approved minimum emergency card. Full emergency access requires authenticated, time-bound break-glass with reason, step-up, audit, notification/review and minimum scope.
- AI is a supervised draft/signal system. It must not autonomously diagnose, prescribe, alter treatment or declare an outbreak.
- AI provider access is server-mediated with no-training/retention/region terms, data minimization, allow-listed models, schema validation, prompt-injection defenses, quotas/cost limits, monitoring, human review and a kill switch.
- Offline clients cannot grant themselves access. Queued commands are encrypted, idempotent, visible and reauthorized on the server.
- Pilot and production environments must not silently fall back to mock database, authentication, cryptography, blockchain or AI success.
- Do not call the application production-ready until release gates are verified.
- Before production PHI, require written Sierra Leone legal and Ministry verification. The April 2026 policy announcement does not by itself prove final enacted obligations.

KNOWN ORIENTATION SIGNALS TO VERIFY, NOT BLINDLY ACCEPT

- credential-like material may be tracked, including Fabric private keys;
- an AI provider key may be exposed through Expo/client configuration and direct client calls;
- demo passwords, fake JWTs or browser session token patterns may exist;
- credentialed CORS may be broadly allowed and raw errors may be returned;
- an audit sync route may lack clear authentication;
- database and Fabric layers may silently simulate success;
- cryptographic verification/recovery may be placeholder behavior;
- the Fabric network may be one organization with TLS disabled while documentation claims multi-organization production behavior;
- ledger structures may reveal stable identifiers or correlatable metadata;
- portals may contain byte-identical authentication, permission, API and mock-data logic;
- automated tests and CI may be absent or insufficient.

Verify each point independently and report when evidence contradicts it.

STOP CONDITIONS

Immediately flag and do not normalize:

- an active exposed credential or private key;
- authentication/authorization bypass;
- cross-patient or cross-facility record access;
- uncontrolled production PHI/AI transfer;
- public or unencrypted clinical storage;
- unverified database/storage authorization;
- paid AI without abuse/cost controls;
- clinical AI without accountable human review;
- inability to restore backups;
- production use without required legal/Ministry approval;
- any Critical finding under the audit standard.

These conditions produce FAIL/STOP SHIP until contained and verified. Do not fix them during Phase 0 unless the human explicitly overrides the audit freeze for an emergency containment action. If emergency authority is granted, document the exact scope and keep containment separate from the remaining audit.
